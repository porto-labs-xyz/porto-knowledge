# PIP-8: Economic Loop

Simple Summary Defines the complete economic loop for Porto: a listener payment enters the Treasury Reserve, the Treasury mints PRT against that cleared payment, the listener's own PRT allotment follows their listening, and finalized stream events settle that value between rights holders, node operators, and the protocol treasury. It also defines the supply, reserve, redemption, staking, fee, and governance.

## Simple Summary

Defines the complete economic loop for Porto: a listener payment enters the Treasury Reserve, the Treasury mints PRT against that cleared payment, the listener's own PRT allotment follows their listening, and finalized stream events settle that value between rights holders, node operators, and the protocol treasury. It also defines the supply, reserve, redemption, staking, fee, and governance relationships that make the loop auditable.

## Abstract

PIP-3 defines PRT issuance, redemption, gas, and reserve custody. PIP-4 defines how a stream becomes a finalized `StreamEvent`. PIP-5 defines listener-attributed settlement and the 70/25/5 network split. PIP-6 defines the bonded operators who earn the operator share. PIP-7 defines governance over the parameters used by those modules.

This PIP connects those mechanisms into one economic model. It is the normative reference for the movement of value through Porto and for the invariants that must remain true across module boundaries. It does not set a price target for PRT, promise a return to any participant, or make PRT a stablecoin.

The central rule is:

> PRT may be minted only against cleared subscription value, and payout value may be allocated only from value that has been attributed to a listener's own finalized listening activity.

## Motivation

Porto's token model has to answer four separate questions without collapsing them into one marketing number:

1. **Where does PRT come from?** From real, cleared subscription payments through the Treasury Module.
2. **Where does demand for PRT come from?** From access to the network, publishing and registration actions, gas, operator staking, and participants choosing to retain PRT exposure.
3. **Who receives the value generated by listening?** The works that the listener actually played, the operators who served and attested those plays, and the protocol treasury according to the governed network split.
4. **How is volatility handled?** Each recipient can choose how much of a payout to retain in PRT and how much to convert to settlement currency at payout time.

Without a single model, these mechanisms can be described independently while leaving ambiguity about double minting, double counting, reserve use, operator compensation, or the difference between PRT's mint-side conversion rate and its floating market price.

## Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Settlement currency** | The fiat or stable settlement unit accepted by the Treasury. Beta initially uses a stablecoin representation such as USDC after payment clearance. |
| **Cleared subscription value** | A listener payment confirmed by the payment processor and transferred or committed to the Treasury Reserve. Reversible, disputed, or failed payments are not cleared value. |
| **Mint-side conversion rate** | The governed rate that converts cleared settlement-currency units into newly minted PRT. It is not the secondary-market price of PRT. |
| **Listener allotment** | The PRT minted from one listener's cleared subscription payment and assigned to that listener's accrual account for settlement. |
| **Core music economy** | The creators, live music, publishing, recorded music, representatives, merchandise, and retail activities included in the economic model. |
| **Epoch** | The accounting period over which a listener's finalized listening activity is accumulated before settlement. Beta defaults to one day. |
| **PRT retirement** | Permanent removal of PRT from circulation by burning it through the governed `BurnRef`. |

## Specification

### 1. Economic loop

The complete flow is:

```text
Listener pays subscription in settlement currency
              |
              v
Payment clears and is credited to the Treasury Reserve
              |
              v
Treasury mints PRT at the governed mint-side conversion rate
              |
              v
PRT is assigned to that listener's accrual account
              |
              v
Finalized StreamEvents record what that listener actually played
              |
              v
Epoch settlement allocates the listener allotment by listening time
              |
              v
Each work allocation splits 70% rights holders / 25% operators / 5% treasury
              |
              v
Recipients retain PRT or convert a chosen portion to settlement currency
```

The payment, mint, accrual, stream attribution, settlement, and payout records must be linked by identifiers that allow an auditor to move from a cleared payment to the finalized works it funded and the final recipients of the resulting allocation.

### 2. Issuance and reserve accounting

For listener `l` and cleared payment `p`, the Treasury calculates:

```text
minted_prt(p) = cleared_settlement_units(p) * mint_rate(epoch) / rate_precision
```

The Treasury must reject a mint when:

- the payment is not marked cleared;
- the payment identifier has already been processed;
- the settlement amount is zero or exceeds the payment processor's authorized bound;
- the conversion rate is outside the governance-controlled rate-change bound; or
- the Treasury Reserve cannot account for the cleared settlement value.

Each mint emits a `MintEvent` containing the payment identifier, listener, settlement amount, conversion rate, minted PRT amount, and epoch. The payment identifier is idempotent across retries and across all settlement-service workers.

The reserve is informational backing for the mint-side conversion and a source of liquidity for the stable-conversion leg of redemption. It is not a redemption peg. The market price of PRT remains free to move above or below the mint-side conversion rate.

The reserve must maintain separate accounting buckets for:

1. **Mint reserve:** settlement value associated with cleared payments that authorized PRT issuance.
2. **Redemption liquidity:** settlement currency available to execute recipients' stable-conversion preferences.
3. **Protocol treasury:** the 5% network share, gas routed to treasury, and other governed protocol revenue.

These buckets may be held by the same custody system, but their balances and movements must be separately observable. The 5% protocol share is not the same thing as reserve backing for newly minted PRT.

### 3. Listener-attributed allocation

The listener allotment created by §2 is credited to a `ListenerAccrual` account. It is not placed into a platform-wide pool.

For listener `l`, epoch `e`, and work `w`, let:

```text
total_duration(l,e) = sum(duration(l,w,e)) for every finalized work w
work_weight(l,w,e) = duration(l,w,e) / total_duration(l,e)
work_allocation(l,w,e) = listener_allotment(l,e) * work_weight(l,w,e)
```

Only finalized `StreamEvent`s from [PIP-4](./PIP-4.md) contribute to `duration(l,w,e)`. Failed, disputed, duplicate, or invalid events do not receive an allocation. The settlement algorithm and gas-bounding rules are specified in [PIP-5](./PIP-5.md).

The total of all work allocations for a listener must equal their listener allotment, subject only to integer rounding. Rounding dust is routed to the protocol treasury and emitted in a settlement event.

### 4. Network split and rights-holder splits

For every finalized work allocation `a`, the governed network split is:

```text
rights_holder_pool = a * 7000 / 10000
operator_pool      = a * 2500 / 10000
treasury_pool      = a - rights_holder_pool - operator_pool
```

The default and current model is:

| Recipient | Share | Economic function |
|---|---:|---|
| Rights holders | 70% | Artists, labels, publishers, and other declared rights holders of the work. |
| Node operators | 25% | Operators who served, attested, and secured the network activity that generated the allocation. |
| Protocol treasury | 5% | Porto protocol development, operations, security, and governed reserve liquidity. |

The 70% rights-holder pool is then split according to the work's declared `rights_holders` vector from [PIP-4](./PIP-4.md). This is a separate layer from the network-level split. A work may have multiple writers, publishers, labels, or other rights holders, and their internal percentages must sum to 10000 basis points.

The 25% operator pool is allocated to the operators that served and attested the relevant finalized activity. During Beta, the sole Trusted Attestor receives the operator allocation according to the disclosed Beta configuration. In Mainnet V1, the operator pool is split across eligible attestors according to the attestation and service accounting defined by [PIP-4](./PIP-4.md) and [PIP-6](./PIP-6.md).

The 5% treasury pool is protocol revenue. It is not an additional rights-holder deduction, a promise to a company, or a second minting source.

### 5. Recipient redemption and PRT retirement

Each recipient may set a `stable_bps` preference from 0 to 10000:

```text
stable_amount = payout_prt * stable_bps / 10000
prt_amount    = payout_prt - stable_amount
```

The stable amount is converted to settlement currency using the payout-time oracle rate described in [PIP-3](./PIP-3.md). The corresponding PRT must be retired through the Treasury `BurnRef` in the same atomic payout path. The remaining PRT is credited to the recipient's wallet.

The default preference for a new rights-holder account is 8000 basis points, or 80% stable conversion, but the account holder may change it. The preference does not change the 70/25/5 allocation; it changes only the recipient's exposure after allocation.

If the oracle is unavailable, outside its deviation bounds, or otherwise invalid, the stable leg must fail closed. The protocol may either defer the stable conversion while retaining the payout as PRT or defer the entire payout according to the governed emergency policy. It must not execute an unpriced conversion.

### 6. Supply dynamics and sinks

Let:

```text
S_t = circulating PRT at the end of period t
M_t = PRT minted against cleared subscription value during t
B_t = PRT retired through gas burn and stable redemption during t
```

Then:

```text
S_t = S_(t-1) + M_t - B_t
```

Transfers between listeners, rights holders, operators, and the treasury do not change total supply. Staking also does not change total supply; it changes the liquidity and slashability of the operator's PRT.

PRT demand and utility are created by:

- listener access and subscription settlement;
- publishing and work-registration actions where fees are enabled;
- stream attestation, payout, governance, and other gas-consuming transactions;
- operator staking and the need to remain eligible for the 25% operator pool;
- recipients choosing to retain PRT rather than convert it; and
- the protocol's use of PRT for network participation and security.

There is no scheduled block reward, discretionary inflation allocation, or marketing grant path in the core token module. Any future incentive program must be separately proposed, transparently funded, and included in the supply equation before activation.

### 7. Worked example

Assume one listener's cleared subscription payment mints 1,000 PRT for an epoch. During that epoch the listener spends 60% of finalized listening time on Work A and 40% on Work B.

| Step | Work A | Work B | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Listener-attributed allocation | 600 PRT | 400 PRT | 1,000 PRT |
| Rights-holder pool, 70% | 420 PRT | 280 PRT | 700 PRT |
| Operator pool, 25% | 150 PRT | 100 PRT | 250 PRT |
| Treasury pool, 5% | 30 PRT | 20 PRT | 50 PRT |

If Work A declares a 50/30/20 rights split, its 420 PRT rights-holder pool becomes 210 PRT, 126 PRT, and 84 PRT for the three declared rights holders. If the first rights holder has an 80% stable preference, 168 PRT is converted and retired through the redemption path and the settlement-currency equivalent is credited; 42 PRT remains in that wallet.

The example demonstrates three properties: the listener's money follows their own listening, the network split is applied after listener attribution, and stable conversion changes the form of a payout without changing the underlying allocation.

### 8. Governed parameters

The parameters below are governed according to [PIP-7](./PIP-7.md). A change to one parameter must not silently change the meaning of another.

| Parameter | Current/default value | Constraint |
|---|---:|---|
| Rights-holder share | 7000 bps | Must remain within the governed split sum. |
| Operator share | 2500 bps | Must remain within the governed split sum. |
| Protocol treasury share | 500 bps | Must remain within the governed split sum. |
| Mint-side conversion rate | Set at launch | Bounded change per epoch; distinct from market price. |
| Stable redemption default | 8000 bps | Account-configurable from 0 to 10000 bps. |
| Beta epoch length | 86,400,000 ms | Must satisfy gas and settlement safety bounds. |
| Maximum works per listener epoch | 200 | Prevents unbounded settlement gas. |
| Gas burn versus treasury routing | 100% burn in Beta registry | Governed within the bounds in PIP-3 and PIP-7. |
| Minimum operator stake | Governance-set | Must be defined before permissionless V1 activation. |
| Unbonding period | 14 days | Must protect pending dispute and slashing windows. |

Governance must publish the old value, new value, effective epoch, reason, and expected supply impact for every tokenomics parameter change. The conversion rate, split, gas policy, and redemption policy should not be changed in the same proposal unless the proposal includes a combined impact analysis.

### 9. Phase behavior

#### Beta

- PRT carries no monetary value.
- A Porto-operated settlement service submits idempotency-keyed mint requests after payment clearance.
- A single Trusted Attestor finalizes stream events.
- A single governance key controls the parameters, subject to on-chain rate bounds.
- The default epoch is daily.
- The sole Trusted Attestor is exempt from permissionless operator staking and slashing while the Beta trust model is active.

#### Mainnet V1

- PRT is minted against real cleared subscription value held in the Treasury Reserve.
- Multiple staked operators attest activity under the m-of-n model.
- The operator pool is allocated across eligible operators according to finalized service and attestation records.
- Governance migrates toward stake-weighted participation as specified by [PIP-7](./PIP-7.md).
- The reserve, treasury, payout, and retirement ledgers are separately observable.

The Beta-to-Mainnet transition must not change the accounting identity in §6 or the meaning of the listener-attributed allocation in §3. It changes the trust and operator-security assumptions underneath the same economic interface.

## Invariants

An implementation conforming to this PIP must preserve these invariants:

1. **Cleared-payment mint invariant.** Every minted PRT amount references exactly one cleared payment identifier and cannot be minted twice for that identifier.
2. **Reserve-accounting invariant.** The settlement value recorded for mint authorization is observable in the mint-reserve ledger and is not silently counted as protocol revenue.
3. **Listener-attribution invariant.** A listener's allotment is allocated only across that listener's own finalized listening activity.
4. **Split invariant.** Every settled work allocation is fully accounted for by the rights-holder, operator, treasury, and explicitly recorded rounding-dust amounts.
5. **Rights-split invariant.** A work's internal rights-holder percentages sum to 10000 basis points before the work can receive settlement.
6. **Supply invariant.** Total circulating supply changes only through authorized minting and authorized PRT retirement.
7. **Redemption invariant.** A stable payout retires the corresponding PRT amount atomically or defers the stable leg without executing an unpriced conversion.
8. **Stake invariant.** Bonding PRT for operator stake locks liquidity but does not create or destroy supply.
9. **Governance-bound invariant.** A governed parameter cannot bypass its declared bounds through a module upgrade or a related parameter update.

## Interfaces and events

PIP-8 does not replace the module interfaces in PIP-3 through PIP-7. It requires those interfaces to expose enough events and views to reconcile the whole loop.

Required cross-module observability includes:

```move
struct EconomicFlowEvent has drop, store {
    payment_id: vector<u8>,
    listener: address,
    epoch: u64,
    minted_prt: u64,
    settled_prt: u64,
    rights_holder_prt: u64,
    operator_prt: u64,
    treasury_prt: u64,
    retired_prt: u64,
}
```

The implementation may emit more granular events instead of this aggregate event, but an indexer must be able to reconstruct the same fields without relying on private off-chain state.

Recommended views include:

```move
#[view] public fun payment_mint_record(payment_id: vector<u8>): MintRecord;
#[view] public fun listener_epoch_allocation(listener: address, epoch: u64): u64;
#[view] public fun work_epoch_allocation(work_id: vector<u8>, epoch: u64): u64;
#[view] public fun reserve_bucket_balances(): (u64, u64, u64);
#[view] public fun supply_accounting(): (u128, u128, u128);
```

## Rationale

**Why model the whole loop in a separate PIP?** PIP-3 through PIP-7 each specify a coherent module boundary, but tokenomics is a cross-module property. A separate PIP gives auditors, integrators, and governance one place to test whether issuance, settlement, payout, staking, redemption, and supply accounting still fit together.

**Why use GVA-style output accounting rather than treating all revenue as value created?** Gross receipts can include goods and services bought from other businesses and can count the same value multiple times across a supply chain. Porto's token loop uses the protocol's own payment and payout accounting, while the economic explanation distinguishes industry output from the bought-in inputs consumed to create it.

**Why keep the listener allotment rather than pool all subscription value?** User-centric settlement makes the source of a payout auditable. It avoids a global popularity pool in which one listener's payment can fund works that listener never played.

**Why separate reserve, redemption liquidity, and protocol treasury?** The reserve authorizes issuance, redemption liquidity serves recipient preferences, and the protocol treasury funds operations. Combining them in one unlabelled balance would make the token's backing and the protocol's revenue impossible to audit cleanly.

**Why retire the PRT used for stable conversion?** A stable conversion removes that PRT exposure from the recipient. Retiring the converted amount prevents the same unit from remaining in circulating supply while also being represented by a stable payout claim.

## Backwards Compatibility

PIP-8 is a cross-cutting specification and does not require a new user-facing asset standard. Existing PIP-3 through PIP-7 implementations remain valid where their behavior satisfies the invariants in the Invariants section. Implementations may need additional accounting events, reserve sub-ledgers, stable-redemption retirement, and reconciliation views to become fully conformant.

Any implementation that currently converts a stable payout without retiring the corresponding PRT must treat that behavior as a migration gap and resolve it before Mainnet V1. Beta may defer stable redemption when the price oracle or reserve liquidity is unavailable.

## Security Considerations

- **Mint replay or forged payment clearance.** A compromised settlement service could attempt to mint without a real payment or mint twice. The Treasury must enforce a designated caller, payment-processor authentication, idempotency, per-epoch volume limits, and reconciliation against the reserve.
- **Reserve insolvency or commingling.** A reserve balance that cannot be reconciled to cleared payments undermines the mint-side value anchor. Bucket-level accounting and independent reconciliation are required.
- **Listener attribution forgery.** Invalid stream events could redirect a listener's allotment. PIP-4's attestation, dispute, content-integrity, and quorum rules must execute before a work receives settlement.
- **Rights metadata manipulation.** A malicious rights-holder vector could redirect the 70% pool. Work registration and rights updates must be authenticated, versioned, and subject to the dispute and governance rules in PIP-4.
- **Operator collusion.** Operators may attempt to fabricate service or attestations to capture the 25% pool. Staking, quorum requirements, availability checks, unbonding, and slashing in PIP-6 are the economic controls.
- **Oracle manipulation.** A bad payout-time price could drain redemption liquidity. Deviation bounds, fail-closed execution, and the Beta single-attestor disclosure in PIP-3 apply.
- **Governance capture.** Governance can change the split, conversion rate, gas policy, and epoch parameters. PIP-7's Beta key controls, rate bounds, migration path, and required parameter disclosure apply.
- **Accounting drift.** The implementation must reconcile minted, allocated, paid, retired, staked, burned, and treasury-routed PRT by epoch. Any unreconciled difference must halt the affected settlement path rather than be silently absorbed.
- **Economic reflexivity.** PRT market price can diverge from the mint-side conversion rate, and no participant should be told that reserve accounting guarantees a market price. User-facing disclosures must distinguish utility, reserve accounting, market price, and protocol revenue.
